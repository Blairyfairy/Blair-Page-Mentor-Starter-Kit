# Mentoring Framework

**By Blair Page**

## Purpose

Mentoring is a collaborative relationship built on curiosity, trust, practical experience, and steady progress. The goal is not to prescribe one career path or provide every answer. It is to help a person clarify what matters, understand options, practice new approaches, and make informed decisions with greater confidence.

## Principles

1. **Listen first.** Understand the mentee's goals, context, strengths, and constraints.
2. **Ask before advising.** Ask whether they would prefer reflective questions, options, candid feedback, or practice.
3. **Make it practical.** Translate broad ambitions into small, observable next steps.
4. **Build independence.** Explain the reasoning behind advice and encourage the mentee to adapt it.
5. **Respect differences.** Make room for different backgrounds, communication styles, access needs, and career paths.
6. **Protect trust.** Agree on confidentiality and its limits before discussing sensitive topics.
7. **Celebrate progress.** Recognize learning, thoughtful experiments, improved communication, and resilience—not only final outcomes.

## First Conversation

### Establish connection
- What would make this mentoring relationship useful to you?
- What would you like me to understand about your current situation?
- How do you prefer to communicate and receive feedback?

### Define success
- If our conversations are helpful, what will be different in 8–12 weeks?
- Which skill, decision, or challenge would you most like to work on?
- How will we recognize progress?

### Explore strengths and opportunities
- What work are you proud of, and what made it successful?
- Which tasks give you energy? Which drain it?
- What feedback have you heard more than once?
- What opportunity would stretch you constructively?

### Agree on working practices
- Set a cadence, session length, and preferred channel.
- Agree how notes and action items will be stored and who can access them.
- Discuss preparation, rescheduling, and how to revisit the agreement.
- Choose one manageable action before the next session.

## Conversation Themes

### Technical problem-solving and systems thinking
- How do you separate symptoms from root causes?
- What evidence would change your hypothesis?
- How would you explain the trade-offs to a nontechnical stakeholder?

### Cloud, infrastructure, and reliability
- What does success look like for availability, performance, security, cost, and recovery?
- Which risk should be tested first?
- What should be documented for the next person?

### Troubleshooting and customer support
- How can you communicate calmly when the answer is not yet known?
- What does the customer need to understand now?
- How will you close the loop and prevent recurrence?

### Communication and stakeholder trust
- Who needs what information, at what level of detail, and by when?
- Which assumptions should be made explicit?
- How can you frame options without overwhelming the audience?

### Leadership and mentoring
- How can you coach without taking over?
- What would help a colleague feel safe asking questions?
- Which repeatable process could make onboarding easier?

### Product, business, and customer value
- Who is the user or customer?
- What problem are we solving?
- How will we know whether the change helped?
- What constraints and trade-offs matter?

### Career positioning and continuous learning
- Which role or direction fits your strengths and values?
- What evidence demonstrates capability?
- What project or practice could help close a skill gap?

### Operations and follow-through
- What is urgent versus important?
- What dependency could block progress?
- What can be simplified, automated, delegated, or documented?

## Suggested Eight-Session Path

1. **Goals and trust:** agree on purpose, expectations, confidentiality boundaries, and one priority goal.
2. **Strengths and evidence:** map strengths, examples of impact, feedback, and growth areas.
3. **Skill or challenge deep dive:** examine one real situation using evidence, root-cause thinking, and alternatives.
4. **Communication practice:** explain a complex topic to different audiences and refine feedback.
5. **Career and opportunity mapping:** compare possible paths, required skills, evidence, and realistic next steps.
6. **Experiment and reflection:** review a project, practice attempt, conversation, or learning activity.
7. **Ownership and resilience:** discuss prioritization, setbacks, boundaries, and asking for help.
8. **Review and continuation:** assess progress, recognize growth, and agree on a self-directed plan.

## Progress Review

Review every three or four sessions:
- Can the mentee describe the goal and why it matters?
- Can they explain their reasoning and identify when support is needed?
- Is there evidence of skill application, such as a work sample or improved process?
- Are communication and follow-through becoming clearer?
- Is the mentee choosing and reviewing their own actions?
- Do they feel heard, respected, and able to disagree?

Ask: What progress are you most proud of? What remains difficult? What should we stop, start, or continue? Has the goal changed? What is your next independent step?
